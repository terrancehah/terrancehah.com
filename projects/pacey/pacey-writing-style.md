# RunAssist — Writing Style Guide

All user-facing text in RunAssist follows these rules. The app speaks as a
calm, measured running coach — not as a system, pipeline, or AI product.

## Voice

Speak as the coach. The user sees a coach, not a pipeline. Never mention
"AI," "GPT," "analysis," "dimensions," "algorithm," or "physiological" in
user-facing text. These are implementation details, not coaching language.

The one exception is marketing copy outside the app experience (meta
descriptions, the project intro tagline) where "AI-powered" is acceptable
as a product descriptor for potential users who haven't entered the app yet.

## Naming

Name things by what the runner sees, not by what the system does. "Race
Readiness" not "AI analysis output." "Pace Breakdown" not "Pace Distribution
vs Goal Pace." "What to Work On" not "Pre Race Insights."

Use "areas of fitness" instead of "dimensions." Use "body signals" instead
of "physiological trends." Use "the coach" instead of "the AI."

## Titles

Keep titles to 2-3 words. If a title needs a subtitle to be understood, the
title is too technical. Titles use sentence case, not title case.

Section titles (h2): Race Goal & Readiness, The Big Picture, Today's
Metrics, Training Trends, Training Log, What to Work On.

Card titles (h3): Race Goal, Race Readiness, Pace Breakdown, Heart Rate vs
Pace, Weekly Mileage, Calendar, Last 5 Activities.

## Subtitles

Subtitles explain what the chart or section shows in one plain-language
sentence. No jargon, no implementation details. If the subtitle is longer
than one sentence, it's trying too hard.

## Labels

Labels are noun phrases, not sentences. "Race Date" not "When is your race."
"Target Pace" not "Pace you need to hit." "Target Time" not "Time Target"
(noun first, more natural English).

Units are part of the value, not the label. "Recovery" not "Recovery Time
(hrs)." The unit "hrs" sits next to the number.

## Consistency

The same field uses the same label everywhere. The onboarding form, the
edit-goal popup, and the goal specifics panel all say "Race Type" (not
"Race Purpose" in one place and "Race Type" in another). Same for "Target
Time" (not "Time Target" in forms and "Target Time" in the panel).

## Errors and status

Errors explain what went wrong and how to fix it, in plain language. They
never apologize and they are never vague. "Failed to load insights." not
"Something went wrong." Status messages say exactly what is happening:
"Sending workouts to Garmin…" not "Processing your request…"

## Buttons

Button text says exactly what happens when you press it, in active voice.
"Send Workouts to Garmin" not "Submit." "Refresh insights" not "Regenerate
Insights." "Save & Continue" not "Next." "Disconnect Garmin" not "Logout."

## What not to do

- Don't expose implementation details (AI, GPT, dimensions, physiological)
- Don't use medical or lab terminology (vitals, analysis, distribution)
- Don't use corporate language (overall insight, regenerate, processing)
- Don't use all caps for labels
- Don't add unnecessary typographic labels above content
- Don't use jargon when a plain word works (areas of fitness, not dimensions)
- Don't apologize in error messages
- Don't use vague status messages
